## Check applicable files rule by rule

**Law:** Take the house rules one at a time, decide which files in `Files:` each rule applies to, check those files (reading the callers and tests the change touches), record the rule's path from its `<!-- rule: <path> -->` line in each finding's `rule` field with what breaks it in `problem`, and populate `machinery[]` for every new or substantially expanded parser, schema/spec validator, serializer, wire client, scheduler, reflection engine, or other general-purpose machinery in `Files:` (`status: broken` with `findingId` referencing a `coding/use-a-maintained-library-before-building-machinery.md` finding, or `status: justified` with `candidatesChecked` naming current maintained major versions and `constraint`), or `machinery: []` when none exists; a rule that applies to none of your files needs no note, a house Law outranks the repo's habits, and a gap excused in a doc, a changelog, or a comment is still a gap.

**When:** Reviewing a stack's files against the loaded house rules.

Bad: Skimming the diff once from memory, skipping a Law because a comment says "known limitation", omitting the `rule` field from a doctrine finding, or leaving a new hand-rolled parser or schema validator out of `machinery[]`.
Good: Walking each rule against every applicable file in `Files:`, setting `rule: invariants/let-the-write-decide-never-a-check-before-it.md` on the finding, recording every new or substantially expanded general-purpose machinery symbol in `machinery[]` (`broken` with `findingId` or `justified` with current maintained major versions in `candidatesChecked` and `constraint`, or `machinery: []` when none exists), and ignoring rules that apply to no file in the list.
